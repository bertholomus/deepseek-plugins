## Findings (most severe first)

1. **Minor (UX, keyboard and screen reader): every action in the panel drops the focus to the page.** Where: `lib/client.js`, `send()` sets `busy`, and every panel button has `disabled: busy` (the main button, Reset, Undo, the presets, the chime switch, the fields). What: the focused button is disabled for the length of the request, and the browser moves the focus to `<body>` (seen after Enter on Start, Pause and Reset, in Playwright's Chromium; the app runs Electron 44.7.0 with Chromium 152, newer than Playwright's, so I expect the same there, though I did not drive the app's own window). A keyboard user who presses Enter on Start and then Enter again gets nothing; Reset then Undo needs two Tabs back into the dialog; a screen reader loses its place (the state line's `aria-live` still speaks). Escape still closes the dialog, so shortcut, Enter, Escape works. DESIGN.md section 9 (keyboard, focus). Fix: do not disable the control that has the focus while busy (ignore its clicks and set `aria-disabled` instead), or remember `document.activeElement` before the request and give the focus back when `busy` ends (to the main button after Start, Pause or Resume; to Undo after a Reset); add an e2e check that the focus is on the main button after Enter on Start and Pause, and on Undo after Reset.
2. **Minor (UX and fail-safe): one click on a length throws a paused session away, with no Undo.** Where: `FocusPanel` (`lockLength = running || busy`, so the presets and Custom are live while paused) and `runOp('set')` in `lib/impl.js` (no `undo` kept). What: a focus paused at 24:43 left became "Short break · ready, 5:00" on one click; there was no Undo row and the host refused `undo`. Reset of the same paused timer offers Undo for a minute, so the safer of two ways to lose a session is the one with the net. Fix: keep the replaced timer for `undo` when `set` replaces a started (paused) timer, as `reset` does, and show "Focus session replaced. [Undo]" in the panel; or lock the lengths while paused too ("Reset to choose another length.").
3. **Minor (docs): the README promises words for an end found late by a window that was closed.** Where: README "A hidden window" ("An end a window learns of more than a minute late (it was closed, or did not know the timer ran) is said the same way, with its time"). What: a window's first read never says an end (`take()` with `seenDone === undefined`; the unit test "a timer that had ended before the window opened is not announced" asserts it). With DSH down across an end and the window opened 79 s later, there was no toast, only the footer mark. Fix: either say an unseen end on a window's first read (`doneSeen === false` and `doneAt` within, say, a few hours, with its time and no chime; the mark would then have words too after a restart), or change the sentence: "A window opened after the end shows the mark on the footer button, whose name says when it ended."
4. **Minor (risk, from the code; not run): in a minimized or covered app window the chime can come up to a minute late.** Where: `lib/client.js` `tick()` (a 1 s `setInterval`, the only way a hidden window learns of the end, since the 5 s read is off while hidden) and `take()` (an end learned over `LATE_MS`, 60 s, is never chimed). What: the app sets no `backgroundThrottling: false` in any of its windows' `webPreferences` (I read them in the app's archive), so Chromium's default throttling of hidden pages applies; for a page hidden over 5 minutes Chromium wakes chained timers once a minute. For a 25-minute focus in a covered window the README's "chimes at the end" can then be up to a minute late, and on the edge not at all. The e2e can only fake `visibilityState` (headless pages are never hidden), so no test sees it. Fix: when a running timer is taken and an AudioContext exists, schedule the two notes on the audio clock at `endAt` (`osc.start(ac.currentTime + (endAt - Date.now()) / 1000)`), cancelled on any change of `rev`; or arm one non-chained `setTimeout` for `endAt` in `take()`; say in the README what holds.
5. **Nit (UX): in DSH's folded rail Focus has no door but the shortcut, and its mark has no seat.** Where: `seatTaken` and the README's Wallpaper section. What: Focus gives its seat to Wallpaper's button (as DESIGN.md asks), so under 1024 px there is no Focus button and no done mark; the toast and chime still say the end. Said honestly in the README; a rail footer with two plugin buttons is DSH's open question. Fix (optional): an item in a DSH menu that exists in the rail, if DSH has a slot for it.
6. **Nit (DESIGN.md section 9, "errors show where the person is looking and take the focus"):** a refusal in the open panel (`data-focus-note`, role alert) is announced but does not take the focus. Fix: focus the note (tabindex -1) or keep the focus on the button that was pressed (finding 1's fix).
